Building Search Visibility From Day One

A website rebuild and SEO foundation for a new multi-service freight and logistics company—turning zero search presence into 9.17K organic clicks in nine months.

The client

One logistics partner, with no search presence yet.

Meridian Freight Group launched in February 2025, bringing freight brokerage, customs brokerage, and internal or fleet shipping under one roof. Their promise was simple: one point of contact instead of three vendors for brokerage, compliance, and freight movement.

01

New company

No established website, domain history, indexed pages, or organic search visibility at launch.

02

Three service lines

Brokerage, customs, and internal shipping each needed their own buyer-focused positioning and search path.

03

One commercial story

The site needed to communicate the value of a single accountable logistics partner without blurring the services together.

04

Inbound growth goal

Meridian wanted a durable demand channel that did not depend entirely on relationships, referrals, and cold outreach.

The challenge

Build authority from zero,
without making the site generic.

A new domain needs time to earn visibility. Meridian also had to serve buyers with very different questions: a shipper sourcing capacity is not evaluating the same information as an importer navigating CBP documentation.

01 / No history

No authority to borrow

There were no rankings or indexed pages to improve—every useful search signal needed to be built.

02 / Different buyers

Different search behavior

Brokerage, customs, and fleet shipping have distinct search language, decision criteria, and compliance concerns.

03 / Clear paths

Fewer dead ends

Visitors needed a direct route from their service need to proof, capabilities, and a relevant next step.

04 / Long game

Compounding visibility

The strategy had to create a durable content and technical foundation instead of chasing broad, low-intent traffic.

05 / Real demand

Commercial intent

Keywords and pages were chosen around the services and markets Meridian wanted to win, not vanity reach.

The solution

A search-ready site and ongoing content program.

BUILD / 01

Service architecture

We built separate, fully developed pages for freight brokerage, customs brokerage, and internal or fleet shipping.

  • Buyer-specific service positioning
  • Capacity, compliance, visibility, and service-area proof
  • Clear paths to the right service
  • Internal linking between related topics
BUILD / 02

Location and lane pages

Instead of a generic “we ship anywhere” claim, the site targeted the routes, locations, and industries Meridian wanted to serve.

  • Location-specific landing pages
  • Lane and market relevance
  • Industry-oriented entry points
  • Relevant conversion paths
BUILD / 03

Technical foundation

The build made it easy for search engines to crawl, understand, and index the growing content library.

  • Fast, accessible pages
  • Schema markup
  • Logical internal linking
  • Clean indexation foundations
GROW / 04

Content and SEO

We paired the build with ongoing keyword research, on-page optimization, and high-intent content development.

  • Freight, brokerage, and customs search research
  • Core-page optimization by buyer need
  • Long-tail commercial content
  • Month-over-month topic expansion

The results

From a new domain to sustained search growth.

Between February and November 2025, Meridian built meaningful organic visibility against established freight competitors. The early months were appropriately quiet while the domain and content foundation gained trust; impressions began climbing in June, and clicks compounded through October and November.

The growth curve

Visibility compounded once the foundation was in place.

Google Search Console data for February through November 2025. By the final months, Meridian was consistently approaching its highest click and impression levels, with the trend still moving upward.

What this demonstrates

New logistics domains can earn their place in search.

There is no instant authority switch. But a clear service architecture, technically sound build, buyer-led pages, and sustained high-intent content can give a new company a credible path to organic demand.

01

Structure matters

Distinct service pages help each buyer find the proof and context relevant to their specific logistics requirement.

02

Intent beats volume

Search work centered on freight, brokerage, and customs questions with commercial relevance—not broad transportation traffic.

03

Consistency compounds

The strongest movement arrived after the initial foundation had time to index, earn trust, and expand with useful content.
