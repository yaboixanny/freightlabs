The logistics ad problem

Freight demand is expensive to misread.

Logistics campaigns operate inside long sales cycles, tight margins, and search results crowded by marketplaces. The account has to separate real shipper demand from expensive noise.

01

Long sales cycles

A form fill is only the beginning. Campaigns need to support the path from first inquiry to quote, lane review, and signed contract.

02

Aggregator pressure

High-intent searches compete with brokers and marketplaces such as uShip and Freightos, making precise positioning essential.

03

Seasonal demand swings

Peak periods, produce seasons, retail cycles, and capacity changes can quickly shift which services and markets deserve budget.

04

Thin-margin pressure

When margins are tight, cost per lead is not enough. Lead quality, quote rate, and expected account value have to guide decisions.

Quick answers

Questions before launch.

01How fast will I see leads?

Campaigns can begin producing inquiries after launch, but timing depends on search volume, budget, geography, offer strength, and account history. Early data is used to improve quality before scaling.

02Do you understand DOT and FMCSA compliance?

We understand the operating context and use it to shape targeting and messaging. Your team remains responsible for approving regulatory statements, authority details, and claims before publication.

03What is your minimum ad spend?

There is no universal minimum. We recommend a viable range after reviewing keyword costs, service area, competition, and the number of campaigns required to produce useful data.

04Do you build landing pages?

Yes. We can create or improve logistics-specific landing pages so each post-click experience matches the search, communicates capability, and qualifies the inquiry.

05Can you track calls and sales outcomes?

Yes. Depending on your systems, tracking can include calls, quote forms, qualified leads, CRM opportunities, offline conversions, and revenue feedback.

06Will you run Performance Max or Local Services Ads?

Only when they fit. Search is often the control layer; Performance Max requires suitable data and creative, while Local Services Ads depend on Google’s category and location eligibility.
