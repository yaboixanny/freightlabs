Cold Email for Freight Forwarders Full Tutorial for 2027

Book a Strategy Call

Cold email can help freight forwarders start conversations with importers and exporters who may need their services. It works best when you contact the right people about trade lanes and shipping needs you can actually support.

This guide covers the steps: choosing your audience, setting up your email, writing a useful message, following up, and measuring the results. Before sending, check the rules that apply where you and your contacts are based.

Article Summary

  • Define the shippers, cargo, and trade lanes that fit your forwarding business.
  • Set up authenticated email accounts and monitor delivery before expanding a campaign.
  • Build a relevant list and check that you have a lawful basis to contact each person.
  • Write short, honest emails with a clear reason for reaching out.
  • Follow up with care, stop when asked, and track qualified conversations.

Start With Your Ideal Shipper Profile

An ideal customer profile (ICP) describes the kind of shipper your freight forwarding company can serve well. Start with current customers: which accounts fit your network, trust your team, and bring work you want more of?

Write down the industries, company size, freight types, shipment volume, origins, and destinations you want to target. Include the services that matter, such as ocean freight, air freight, customs support, or temperature-controlled shipping. Then identify the people who influence the choice of forwarder, such as logistics, import, export, and supply chain managers.

A clear ICP helps your team avoid a long list of poor-fit contacts. It also gives each message a reason to exist. For a wider view of positioning and buyer research, explore our marketing services for freight forwarders.

Set Up Your Email Before Sending

A relevant message cannot help if it never reaches the inbox. Work with your email provider or IT team to set up your sending accounts and confirm that SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are configured correctly. These settings help receiving mail systems check that messages are genuinely sent by your domain.

  • Use a recognizable sender: Your name, domain, and company details should make it clear who is contacting the reader.
  • Plan your sending setup: Some teams use a separate, clearly branded domain or subdomain for outreach. This does not guarantee protection for your main domain or your brand’s reputation.
  • Start small: Send to a limited group first and increase volume only when the list, responses, and delivery support it. There is no universal safe daily limit.
  • Watch for problems: Review delivery errors, spam complaints where available, and replies. Pause and investigate if problems rise.

Do not rely on automated “warm-up” tools or a fixed number of messages to guarantee inbox placement. Follow your provider’s current sending rules and focus on reaching people who have a real reason to hear from you.

Build a List of Shippers Who Fit

Choose a small group of companies that match your ICP. Look for a clear link between their business and your services: a market they serve, an import or export location, or the type of goods they move. Use public company information and reliable business data, then check each record before adding it to your campaign.

Confirm the person’s role and the accuracy of the address. Email verification can reduce obvious errors, but it cannot prove that a person wants your message or that you have permission to send it. Keep a list of people who opt out, ask not to be contacted, or are not suitable prospects, and check new lists against it.

Write a Freight Forwarding Cold Email

Make your first email easy to read. Say why you chose the company, explain one relevant way you may be able to help, and ask a simple question. Use details you can verify. Avoid claims about results, customers, or rates unless they are true and you have permission to share them.

Example: a short first email

Subject: Your shipments from Asia

Hi [First name],

I noticed [Company] imports [product] from [market]. We help shippers manage ocean freight on [trade lane], including [specific service you provide].

Is reviewing support on that lane a priority for your team this quarter?

[Your name]
[Company and contact details]

Only use the example if its details are accurate and you may lawfully contact the recipient. Personalize the subject, lane, and service to fit the account. Include the identification and opt-out information required for the campaign.

Plan a Respectful Follow-Up

One message may be missed, but a long series of repeated pitches can damage trust. If follow-up is allowed, plan a short sequence over a few weeks. Give each message a reason: clarify the lane you serve, share a useful resource, or ask whether another person handles freight decisions.

Stop the sequence when someone replies, opts out, or asks not to be contacted. Do not add them to a new campaign just because they did not answer. If someone says “not now,” ask whether and when they would welcome a future conversation, then record that preference.

Use LinkedIn Alongside Email

LinkedIn can help you understand a company and find the right person. A useful post or a genuine connection can support a conversation, but it should not become another stream of repeated pitches. Coordinate with your team so a prospect is not contacted by several people at once. Follow platform rules and the laws that apply to direct messages too.

Track Results That Matter

Start by tracking delivery problems, replies, positive replies, qualified conversations, quotes, and customers. Review which industries and trade lanes produce the best fit. Open rates can be unreliable, so do not treat them as proof that a prospect read your message.

There is no dependable reply-rate benchmark for every forwarder. Your audience, market, list quality, offer, and legal limits all affect results. Compare campaigns against your own starting point and use sales feedback to improve the next group.

Common Cold Email Mistakes

  • Contacting people without checking whether the message is allowed.
  • Sending the same broad pitch to every shipper, regardless of trade lane or freight type.
  • Using made-up customer results, fake reply threads, or misleading subject lines.
  • Ignoring delivery errors, opt-outs, and requests to stop.
  • Asking for a meeting before explaining why the conversation is relevant.
  • Measuring success by messages sent instead of good-fit sales opportunities.

Choose Tools That Support Your Process

You may need a business email provider, a way to research contacts, an email verification tool, a system for managing outreach, and a CRM to record replies and next steps. Pick tools that fit your team and your budget. Check their security, privacy, suppression, and reporting features before sharing contact data.

Check Cold Email Rules Before You Send

The rules depend on where you send from, where the recipient is, and the type of contact. Business-to-business email is not automatically exempt. Get advice for your campaign before you send, and keep records of the basis for contact and any opt-outs.

  • United States: The FTC’s CAN-SPAM guide covers truthful headers and subject lines, a valid postal address, a clear opt-out, and honoring opt-outs within 10 business days.
  • Canada: CRTC guidance on CASL explains that commercial messages generally require consent, sender identification, and an unsubscribe method, subject to specific exceptions. A business email address alone is not blanket permission to send.
  • United Kingdom: The ICO’s business-to-business marketing guidance explains that email and data protection rules depend on the recipient and how personal data is used. Other countries can have different requirements.

This overview is a starting point, not legal advice. Have a qualified adviser review the rules for each market you target.

Build a Better Freight Forwarding Pipeline

Start with one audience and one service you know well. Check the rules, build a focused list, and test a clear message. Review the replies with your sales team, improve the campaign, and grow only when you can see what is working.
