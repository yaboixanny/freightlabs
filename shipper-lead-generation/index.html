The account problem

A company can ship freight and still be the wrong prospect.

Useful shipper prospecting starts with the freight your brokerage can serve profitably—not a database category or a job title alone.

01

No lane connection

The account moves freight, but its origins, destinations, or mode do not support your network.

02

The facility is unclear

Headquarters data hides the plants, warehouses, ports, and distribution points where transportation decisions begin.

03

One contact is not a buying group

Transportation, procurement, operations, and finance can each influence whether a new broker gets considered.

04

Timing is invisible

A good account may have no immediate need until capacity, service, volume, or network conditions change.

What creates a reason to talk

Use signals that can change a transportation plan.

The right message connects a visible business event to a credible way your brokerage can help.

SIGNAL / 01

Facility and market expansion

New plants, warehouses, distribution regions, or product lines can introduce unfamiliar lanes and capacity needs.

SIGNAL / 02

Seasonal and promotional volume

Predictable peaks create moments when primary routing guides and existing capacity may need support.

SIGNAL / 03

Mode or equipment pressure

Specialized trailers, cross-border moves, urgent freight, and difficult commodities can expose a specific service gap.

SIGNAL / 04

Service disruption

Coverage failures, missed appointments, poor visibility, or changing incumbents can create an opening for a qualified alternative.

Shipper prospecting questions

What freight broker teams ask.

01How is this different from buying a shipper list?

A purchased list groups companies by broad attributes. An account map starts with your real freight criteria, identifies relevant operating facilities, maps the buying group, and records why each account may fit.

02Can you target specific lanes?

Yes. We can start with priority origins, destinations, regions, modes, equipment, and commodities, then research accounts whose operating footprint supports that network thesis.

03Who counts as a transportation buyer?

The buying group varies by company. It may include transportation and logistics leaders, procurement, plant or warehouse operations, supply-chain executives, and finance stakeholders.

04What makes an account sales-ready?

Sales-readiness means there is a credible operating fit and enough context to continue: relevant freight, an identified need or timing signal, the right stakeholder, and a useful next step.

05Does this replace broad lead generation?

No. This is the narrower account-based option for brokerages that know the freight they want. Our broader freight broker lead generation service can cover a wider outbound program.
